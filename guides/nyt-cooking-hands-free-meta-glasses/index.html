NYT Cooking Hands-Free on Meta glasses.

New York Times Cooking can now read a recipe to you through Meta AI glasses, one step at a time, and show the steps in the lens on Meta Ray-Ban Display. Here is what it needs, how to set it up, and what to do when it will not work for you.

Updated November 17, 2026 By Malik Shourbaji, founder of Glutt 4 min read

Short answer

NYT Cooking Hands-Free reads recipe steps through Meta AI glasses and moves on when you say "next." It needs an iPhone 15 or later on iOS 26 or later with Apple Intelligence on, the Meta AI app connected to your glasses, and a recipe that supports it: a selection of NYT recipes to start. It is not available on iPad. To start, open a supported recipe and choose Hands-Free in its Preparation section. If your phone is older, you are on iPad, or the recipe is not from NYT, Glutt talks you through any recipe you have saved through the same glasses, on iOS 17.2 or later.

What NYT Cooking Hands-Free does

It turns a supported NYT recipe into spoken steps. NYT's editors rewrote long steps into short instructions with the ingredients and amounts built in, so you are not listening to a paragraph read out word for word. 2 There are two versions, depending on your glasses: 1

What you need

  • An iPhone 15 or later. It is not available on iPad.
  • iOS 26 or later, with Apple Intelligence turned on. Check Settings if you are not sure.
  • The Meta AI app, updated and connected to your glasses. Glasses software updates also happen there.
  • Bluetooth and microphone access for NYT Cooking, so it can reach the glasses and hear you.
  • A supported recipe. Hands-Free works on a selection of NYT recipes to start, with more being added.

NYT's help page also asks that the glasses are switched on, worn, and have their arms fully unfolded. 1

How to set it up

  1. Open a recipe that supports Hands-Free in the NYT Cooking app. The first time, a prompt offers to connect your glasses; if it does not appear, tap Hands-Free in the recipe's Preparation section.
  2. Allow NYT Cooking to find Bluetooth devices, and accept the terms.
  3. The Meta AI app opens and asks to connect NYT Cooking to your glasses. Approve it, and you are sent back.
  4. Allow microphone access. Your glasses show up as connected.
  5. Tap Start Hands-Free, then cook. Say "next", "back" or "help" to move around, and "exit" to stop.

Why is the Hands-Free option missing?

  • The recipe is not supported yet. Only some recipes have it. NYT points to collections such as its most popular recipes and quick weeknight dinners as good places to look.
  • The app cannot see your glasses. The option only appears when NYT Cooking detects connected glasses: check they are on, connected in the Meta AI app, and in Bluetooth range.
  • Your phone does not qualify. An iPhone 14 or older, an iPad, or Apple Intelligence switched off will keep it hidden.

When NYT Hands-Free is not the right fit

If you cook from NYT Cooking on a recent iPhone, use it: the step rewrites are genuinely good. It is a narrator for NYT's own catalogue, though, so three groups are left out:

  • Older iPhones and iPads. Anything below an iPhone 15, or any iPad, cannot run it.
  • Recipes from anywhere else. A TikTok you saved, a family recipe, a food blog: Hands-Free only reads supported NYT recipes.
  • Cooks with questions. It moves you through steps. It does not answer "can I use butter instead?" or "is this browned enough?"

That is the gap Glutt fills with the same glasses. Glutt uses them as a Bluetooth headset, so Polly, its AI chef, talks you through any recipe you saved, from TikTok and Instagram, YouTube, websites or screenshots, keeps your place, runs timers, and answers questions out loud. It runs on iOS 17.2 or later. It does not draw anything on the Display's screen; that part is NYT's advantage today.

Frequently asked questions

Does NYT Cooking work with Ray-Ban Meta glasses?

Yes. NYT Cooking Hands-Free reads supported recipes aloud through Ray-Ban Meta, Oakley Meta HSTN, Oakley Meta Vanguard and other Meta AI glasses, and shows the steps in the lens on Meta Ray-Ban Display. It needs an iPhone 15 or later on iOS 26 with Apple Intelligence on.

Why don't I see Hands-Free on an NYT Cooking recipe?

Either the recipe is not supported yet (only a selection are), NYT Cooking cannot detect your glasses (check they are on, connected in the Meta AI app and in Bluetooth range), or your device does not qualify: it needs an iPhone 15 or later on iOS 26 with Apple Intelligence, and it is not available on iPad.

What voice commands does NYT Cooking Hands-Free use?

Say "next," "back" or "help" to move through the recipe, and "exit" to leave Hands-Free mode.

Can I use NYT Cooking Hands-Free on an iPad or an older iPhone?

No. NYT says it needs an iPhone 15 or later and is not available on iPad. Glutt works with Meta glasses as Bluetooth audio on iOS 17.2 or later and talks you through any recipe you saved.

Can NYT Cooking Hands-Free read recipes from other websites?

No. It reads supported NYT Cooking recipes. To cook a recipe from TikTok, Instagram, YouTube or another site hands-free through Meta glasses, import it into an app like Glutt, whose AI chef reads the steps and answers questions.
